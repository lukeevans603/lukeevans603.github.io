About

Management Data into Decisions

I'm a quantitative ecologist who works at the applied end of conservation. After a Ph.D. on human-crocodile conflict in Malaysian Borneo and postdoctoral positions at Stanford University, Arizona State University, and the University of Florida, my work now centers around aiding decision makers in making informed management strategies.

The answers have been statewide crocodile population models for the Sabah government, invasion risk platforms for the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission and the Department of the Interior, and a live evaluation of the National Park Service's twelve-year tegu removal programme in the Everglades. In every case the product is a tool with a user, not a report with a recommendation.

Getting to adoption means workshops, expert elicitation, training, and time with the people who will use the tool. That side of the work, where science starts changing what a manager does on Monday morning, is the part I find most satisfying.

Programs

Research Programs

Applied work built around management questions, on a foundation of a decade of field ecology in Borneo.

Invasive & Exotic Wildlife

Quantitative risk assessment for the exotic pet trade, climate adaptation of invasion risk, and evaluation of long-running removal programmes. Risk platforms built for the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission and the Department of the Interior are in daily use. A Bayesian state-space framework now tracks catch rates, recruitment, and population trend across twelve years of Argentine black and white tegu removal for the National Park Service.

Human-Wildlife Conflict & Coexistence

A decade of work on saltwater crocodiles and Bornean elephants, from satellite tracking and population genetics to the statewide crocodile action plan that underpins Sabah's CITES non-detriment finding. Current work brings population and harvest modelling to conflict, so that management decisions can be weighed against their likely effect on both wildlife and people.

Working Lands & Habitat

Remote sensing to find where habitat gain costs a landowner least. In Borneo, oil palm land that flooded regularly and yielded poorly was identified from LiDAR and satellite imagery, and retiring it reconnected isolated forest at a saving to the grower. The same approach applies wherever habitat and agriculture share a landscape, using satellite time series and LiDAR to find the ground that gives the most back to wildlife for the least cost to production.

Protected Areas & Movement Ecology

Pairing animal movement with high-resolution habitat mapping to set conservation priorities. Research on Bornean elephant connectivity and forest carbon contributed to the creation of a 400,000-hectare protected area in Sabah, and continues in spatial prioritisation for wildlife corridors and mixed-use landscapes.

Borneo Deforestation

This visualization shows the dramatic decline in forest cover across Borneo from 2000 to 2023. Understanding these landscape-level changes is central to my research on wildlife connectivity, habitat fragmentation, and conservation prioritization in Southeast Asia.
